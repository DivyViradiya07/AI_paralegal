1] Rajiv Sharma, a transport contractor, is accused of underreporting assets under Section 69A of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The Income Tax Department claims that Rajiv lifted large quantities of construction materials but failed to deliver the entire amount. He presents photocopies of delivery challans as proof, but the authorities argue that some of the receipts are forged. The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal rules in favor of the department, but Rajiv wants to challenge this decision in the Supreme Court. How can Rajiv prove that he was not the owner of the missing goods and avoid additional tax liability?

2] Meena Patel owns farmland near an upcoming industrial area. The government acquires her land under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894, offering ₹10 per square meter. However, she learns that nearby landowners received ₹30 per square meter for similar land. Meena challenges the compensation in court, arguing that her land has high potential value and should be compensated accordingly. The High Court partially increases the compensation, but she believes it is still undervalued. What factors determine fair market value in land acquisition cases?

3] ABC Builders Ltd. takes a 90-year lease of land from the New Okhla Industrial Development Authority (NOIDA) for a housing project. Due to financial troubles, ABC Builders defaults on payments and enters the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016. NOIDA claims that it is a financial creditor and should have priority in recovering dues. However, the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) rules that NOIDA is an operational creditor, not a financial one. Can NOIDA claim financial creditor status for lease payments?

4] XYZ Ltd. borrows ₹50 crore from PQR Finance and pledges 5 million shares as collateral. The company defaults on the loan, and PQR Finance invokes its right to sell the pledged shares. However, the company argues that the Depositories Act, 1996, and SEBI regulations require additional procedures before the shares can be transferred. PQR Finance files a legal claim to enforce its rights. Can PQR Finance sell the pledged shares without notifying XYZ Ltd.?
5] Anjali buys a commercial property from Rahul, who claims to be the rightful owner. However, a charitable trust later claims that the property was part of a court decree in their favor, and Anjali’s purchase is invalid. The trust moves the Executing Court to remove Anjali from the property, citing Order XXI Rules 97-102 of CPC. Anjali argues that she is a bona fide purchaser with valid ownership rights. Can Anjali challenge the execution of the decree against her property?
